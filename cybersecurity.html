What a cybersecurity assessment covers

  • Who has access to what, and whether that access still makes sense
  • Where sensitive data lives, and how well it's protected in each place
  • What's outdated, unpatched, or unsupported
  • Whether backups actually restore, not just exist
  • What would happen, step by step, if one account were compromised today

Assessment now, monitoring ongoing

A one-time assessment tells you where you stand today. If you want that coverage to continue — catching new risks as they emerge rather than waiting for the next scheduled check — that's part of what our managed IT services cover.

What happens after an assessment

You get a prioritized list, not a wall of anxiety. Some fixes — turning on MFA, closing an unused account — can happen the same week. Bigger issues get scoped as a proper project with a clear timeline.

Questions

Cybersecurity — FAQs

Q

How is an audit different from ongoing monitoring?

An audit is a point-in-time check. Ongoing monitoring, part of managed IT, catches new risks as they come up rather than only at the next scheduled audit.

Q

What does an assessment actually check?

Access, data location, outdated software, backup integrity, and what happens if one account is compromised.

Q

We're small — are we really a target?

Most breaches at small businesses come from ordinary gaps, not targeted attacks. Size doesn't exempt you from the basics.

Q

What happens after an assessment?

A prioritized list — quick fixes can happen the same week, bigger issues get scoped as a project.
