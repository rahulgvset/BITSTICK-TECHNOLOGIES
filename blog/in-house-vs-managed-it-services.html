In-House IT vs Managed IT Services: A Direct Comparison

By Bitstick Technologies Team · Published August 25, 2026 · 7 min read

Every growing business eventually has this conversation: do we hire someone in-house for IT, or bring in a managed service provider? Both are legitimate answers — the right one depends on your size, budget, and how much day-to-day, in-person presence actually matters to you.

The direct comparison

Factor In-house hire Managed IT services
Cost structureFixed salary + benefits + overhead, regardless of workloadScoped to coverage level, usually lower than a full-time hire for small teams
Coverage breadthLimited to one person's skill setAccess to a team with varied specialties
Single point of failureYes — sick days, leave, and turnover create real gapsNo — coverage continues regardless of any one person's availability
Day-to-day presenceIn your office, immediate physical availabilityRemote, usually with defined response times
Institutional knowledgeBuilds up over time with one dedicated personDocumented and shared across a team, less dependent on one individual
ScalingRequires hiring more people as needs growCoverage scales with your agreement, not a hiring process

The comparison isn't really "which is better" — it's "which is a better fit for your size and risk tolerance right now."

When in-house makes more sense

  • You're large enough that IT work genuinely fills a full-time role
  • Physical, immediate presence matters — hardware-heavy environments, for example
  • You want someone deeply embedded in company culture and daily operations

When managed IT makes more sense

  • You're too small to fully utilize a full-time hire, but still need real coverage
  • You want breadth — security, cloud, networking — that one person is unlikely to cover equally well
  • Continuity matters more than physical presence — you can't afford a gap when someone's out

The middle option: co-managed IT

Many businesses don't have to choose one or the other. Co-managed IT pairs a lean in-house presence — someone who knows your team and handles daily requests — with a managed provider covering monitoring, security, and specialist work the in-house person doesn't have bandwidth for. This is often the right answer for businesses that have outgrown "no IT support" but aren't ready for a full internal department. See our managed IT services page for how this works in practice.

Questions

In-house vs managed IT — FAQs

Q

Is managed IT cheaper than hiring in-house?

Usually, for businesses too small to fully utilize a full-time hire. The comparison flips at a certain size.

Q

Can we do both?

Yes — this is co-managed IT, a lean in-house presence paired with a managed provider for depth and coverage.

Q

What's the biggest risk of in-house only?

A single point of failure — coverage drops to zero if your one IT hire is unavailable.

Q

What's the biggest risk of fully managed?

Less day-to-day, in-person presence compared to someone sitting in your office full-time.
